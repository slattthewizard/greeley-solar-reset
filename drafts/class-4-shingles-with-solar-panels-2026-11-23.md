# Class 4 Shingles With Solar Panels: What to Know Before the Reroof

If you live in Weld County, you already know what hail does. Your roof is coming off because of it, or because of age, and now your roofer is asking whether you want to step up to impact-resistant shingles. You also have solar panels sitting on that roof, and you'd like to know how the two fit together.

Choosing class 4 shingles with solar panels on the house is a perfectly reasonable move, and it's one we see homeowners consider a lot in this part of Colorado. This guide covers how the shingle choice interacts with the detach and reset, what it protects, and what it doesn't.

One thing up front. We don't sell or install roofing. Your roofer handles the shingles. Our part is the panels: taking them off, storing them, and putting them back on new flashed mounts after the roof is done. This article is about coordinating the two.

## What "Class 4" actually means

Impact-resistant shingles are rated under a test standard called UL 2218. It grades roofing materials by how well they hold up when steel balls are dropped on them, and Class 4 is the top rating. A Class 4 shingle has passed the toughest version of that test.

A few honest limits on what that means. The rating is a lab result. It tells you how the product performed under that specific test, not that the shingle can't be damaged. Very large hail, or hail driven by wind, can still harm a rated roof. And impact-resistant shingles are built to resist the kind of damage that causes cracks, bruising, and granule loss. They're not a promise that a roof comes through every storm untouched.

Still, in one of the most hail-prone parts of the country, many homeowners decide the extra resistance is worth considering.

## Do Class 4 shingles work with solar mounts?

Yes, in general. Impact-resistant asphalt shingles are installed on the roof deck the same way as ordinary shingles, and a solar array attaches through the shingles to the structure below with flashed mounts. The mount is a separate component from the shingle, and flashing is designed to keep water out at that attachment point.

That said, a few practical things deserve a conversation between your roofer and your solar contractor:

- **Shingle thickness and stiffness.** Some impact-resistant products are heavier or stiffer than standard shingles. Flashing needs to sit and seal correctly over and under them, so ask your roofer how the product handles around penetrations.
- **Flashing details.** Your roofer and the solar crew should agree on how the flashing at each mount integrates with the new shingle courses.
- **Warranty language.** Some roofing warranties have conditions about penetrations. We cover that in our post on [whether a detach and reset voids a solar warranty](/blog/does-detach-reset-void-solar-warranty/).

None of this is a barrier. It's a reason to make sure the two trades talk before the work starts.

## The panels don't get any tougher

This is the point people miss, so here it is. Putting class 4 shingles under your array doesn't make the solar panels more hail resistant. The panels are a separate product with their own rating.

Panels sold in the US are typically certified to IEC 61215 and UL 61730, and the hail test in those standards fires 25 mm (1 inch) ice balls at about 23 m/s, roughly 51 mph. That's a baseline. Hail larger than that, or driven harder, can crack the glass or the cells underneath, sometimes with no visible sign. Our post on [solar panel hail rating](/blog/solar-panel-hail-rating/) breaks down what that certification does and doesn't mean.

So if you're comparing roofing options in hopes of protecting the whole system, understand the split. The shingles protect the roof. The panels protect themselves, within their rating. If a bad storm hits, you may need your array inspected whether your shingles held up or not. Some insurers offer a premium discount for homes with impact-resistant roofing. Whether yours does, how much it is, and what proof they want are all questions only your carrier can answer. We aren't insurance advisers, and we won't quote you a figure.

Here's a sensible way to handle it:

1. **Call your insurance agent before you pick a shingle.** Ask whether they offer any discount for Class 4 roofing and what documentation they need.

## FAQ

### Can I put class 4 shingles on a roof with solar panels?

Yes. Impact-resistant shingles go on the roof deck the way standard shingles do, and the array is reattached afterward with flashed mounts. Ask your roofer how the product handles around penetrations, and let your roofer and solar contractor agree on flashing details before the work starts.

### Do Class 4 shingles protect my solar panels from hail?

No. The shingles protect the roof, and the panels have their own rating. Panels sold in the US are typically certified to IEC 61215 and UL 61730, which includes a hail test using 25 mm ice balls at about 51 mph. Larger or harder hail can still damage panels regardless of what is under them.

### Will Class 4 shingles lower my homeowner's insurance?

Some insurers offer a premium discount for impact-resistant roofing, and some don't. We can't say what yours does. Call your agent before you choose a shingle and ask whether a discount exists and what proof of installation they require.

### Does a Class 4 roof change the cost of a solar detach and reset?

The shingle type isn't the main driver. Panel count, roof pitch, access, and racking type matter most. We quote every job in writing after looking at the array, so you'll see the exact line items before anything is scheduled.

### Should I tell my roofer I have solar before choosing a shingle?

Yes, and do it early. Your roofer needs to plan around the array, coordinate flashing at the mount locations, and check whether their warranty has conditions about solar. A quick conversation between the roofer and the solar contractor before the roof is scheduled avoids delays.
