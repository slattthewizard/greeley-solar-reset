# Does Removing Solar Panels Void the Warranty?

It's the question that comes up in nearly every reroof conversation. Your roofer says the panels have to come off, and you wonder whether that just cancelled the coverage on a system that cost you a lot of money.

The honest answer is that it depends on which warranty you mean, and on what your particular documents say. There isn't one solar warranty. There are usually several, held by different companies, and a detach and reset touches each one differently. We'll walk through them so you know what to read and who to call.

We can't tell you what your warranty says. We don't know your terms, and guessing would do you no favors. What we can do is show you where the real risks sit.

## The four warranties on a typical solar roof

Most solar homes with a roof replacement coming have some combination of these:

1. **Manufacturer product and performance warranties.** The panel maker and the inverter maker each stand behind their own equipment. The product side covers defects. The performance side covers how much power the panels should still produce over time.
2. **Installer workmanship warranty.** This is the installing company's promise about the install itself, including the wiring and the way the system was attached to your roof.
3. **Roof penetration warranty.** Where the installer drilled through your roof to mount the array, some contractors offer a separate promise that those holes won't leak.
4. **Roofer's new-roof warranty.** When you replace the roof, the roofing company usually warrants its own work and the materials it installs.

These don't automatically overlap. A claim on one doesn't mean you can claim on another. That's why it helps to know which is which before work begins.

## Manufacturer warranties and removal

Panel and inverter warranties are written around the equipment. The usual concern is whether moving the equipment changes the terms. Some manufacturer documents include language about who may handle or reinstall the product, how it should be handled, or what happens to coverage if the unit is damaged during handling.

We can't speak for any specific manufacturer, and we don't claim any manufacturer certification. Read the document itself, or call the manufacturer and ask directly: "I'm removing and reinstalling my array for a roof replacement. Does that affect my warranty, and are there handling or installer requirements?" Ask them to answer in writing if you can.

One more thing worth knowing. A panel that gets cracked or damaged while being handled is a different matter from a panel with a factory defect. Careful handling, proper staging, and photographs before and after all help show which is which. ## The installer's workmanship warranty

If your original installer is still around, their workmanship warranty is the one most likely to be affected when someone else touches the system. Some installer contracts say that work by another party can change or end the coverage. Others don't mention it. Again, the contract decides.

Before the panels come off, it's smart to contact the installer, tell them what's planned, and ask whether they'd rather do the detach and reset themselves or whether they have any requirements for a third party. You'll get a clear answer, and you'll have the exchange on record.

If your installer has closed, the workmanship coverage may already be gone. That's covered in our post on what to do when your [solar installer is out of business](/blog/solar-installer-out-of-business/).

## The roof penetration warranty and the new mounts

This is where a reroof actually changes things for the better. When panels come off, the old mounts and their penetrations are exposed. At a reset, we install new flashed mounts at the attachment points rather than reusing the old penetrations.

That matters for warranties because the new mounts are new work. Whoever installs them is the one who warrants them. In practice, it means the roof penetration promise from the original install generally doesn't carry over to the new attachment points, because those points didn't exist before. The new ones are covered by whoever did the work, and that should be spelled out in writing in the contract you sign.

Ask any contractor you're considering: who warrants the new mounts, for how long, and what does the document say? We're not going to quote you a term here. Ask for it on paper, and compare it with what the roofer is offering. Our post on [roof leaks under solar panels](/blog/roof-leak-under-solar-panels/) goes into why attachment points are where leaks begin.

## The roofer's new-roof warranty

Many roofing warranties include language about work done on the roof by others. A typical concern is that anything attached to or through the new roofing after the roofer leaves could change the coverage. That's why the order of work matters.

The cleanest approach is to have the roofer know the solar plan up front. The panels come off, the new roof goes on, and the reset happens afterward on top of it. Ask your roofer to read the warranty language about solar reattachment and tell you whether they require anything, such as a particular flashing detail or a specific contractor. ## Why documenting the array before removal matters

If there's one habit that protects you under every warranty at once, it's documentation. Before anything gets disconnected, you want a record of the system as it stands.

- **Photographs of the whole array,** from the ground and, where safe, close up, including every panel.
- **Photos of the inverter, the disconnect, the meter, and the wiring runs.**
- **A screenshot of production data** from your monitoring app, so there's a baseline.
- **A list of panel serial numbers,** which are on the labels on the back of each panel and in some monitoring portals.
- **Your warranty documents,** kept together with the above.

Why does this matter? If a panel is later found cracked, or the system produces less than before, a manufacturer or an installer may ask what the condition was before the work. A photo taken before is worth more than any explanation after. If production does dip, our post on [solar panels producing less after reinstall](/blog/solar-panels-producing-less-after-reinstall/) helps you tell normal from not normal.

A reputable contractor will document some of this as part of the job, and every reset we do ends with a production test. Keep your own copy anyway.

## The short list of questions to ask

Before you sign anything, ask these and get the answers in writing:

- To the manufacturer: does removal and reinstall change my product or performance warranty?
- To the original installer: does work by another contractor affect my workmanship warranty?
- To the new contractor: who warrants the new mounts and the reinstall, and what does the document say?
- To the roofer: does reattaching solar over the new roof affect your warranty, and do you have any requirements?

## FAQ

### Does removing solar panels void the manufacturer warranty?

Not by itself in every case, but it depends on the wording of your specific warranty. Some manufacturer documents include handling or installer requirements. Read yours, or ask the manufacturer directly whether removal and reinstall for a roof replacement affects coverage, and ask for the answer in writing.

### Will my installer's workmanship warranty still apply after a detach and reset?

That depends on your installer's contract. Some installers say that work by another party changes or ends the coverage, and others don't address it. Contact your installer before the panels come off and ask. If the company has closed, that warranty may already be gone.

### Who warrants the new mounts after a reset?

Whoever installs them. A reset uses new flashed mounts at the attachment points, so those are new work, and the contractor doing the reset is the one who warrants them. Ask for the terms in writing before you sign, and compare them to what your roofer offers on the new roof.

### Should I take photos before my panels are removed?

Yes. Photograph the full array, the inverter, the disconnect, and the meter, and save a screenshot of your production data. Note the panel serial numbers too. If a question about damage or output ever comes up, a record from before the work is the strongest thing you can show.

### Does a new roof warranty change if solar is put back on top?

It can, depending on the roofer's warranty language. Many roofers have specific terms about work done by others on the new roof. Ask your roofer to read the warranty with the solar reattachment in mind and tell you whether they require a particular flashing detail or contractor.
