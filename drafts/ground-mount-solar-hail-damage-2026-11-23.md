# Ground Mount Solar Hail Damage: What to Check on Your Acreage Array

If you live on acreage around Eaton or out in rural Weld County, there's a good chance your solar sits on the ground, or on the roof of a barn or shop, and not on the house. That changes some things after a hailstorm and leaves others exactly the same. This page covers ground mount solar hail damage: what to look at, what's easier than a rooftop, and where the insurance question gets different.

We're not insurance advisers, so on coverage we'll point you to your policy and your carrier. On the physical side, we'll tell you what we look for.

## Same hail, different setup

Hail doesn't care where a panel is mounted. A ground array takes the same stones as a roof array, and Weld County sits in one of the most hail-prone parts of the country. Panels sold in the US are typically certified to IEC 61215 and UL 61730, whose hail test fires 25 mm (1 inch) ice balls at about 23 m/s, roughly 51 mph. Plenty of Northern Colorado storms exceed that test, in stone size, in wind-driven speed, or both.

The difference is geometry. Ground arrays are often tilted at a steeper angle than a roof, and they sit in open fields, where there's nothing to block wind-driven hail from the side. For what that rating does and doesn't promise, see our post on the [solar panel hail rating](/blog/solar-panel-hail-rating/).

## What's easier on a ground mount

Inspection. You can walk up to a ground array. You can see the glass at eye level, check the underside, look at every connection, and photograph all of it without a ladder or a harness. That's a real advantage after a storm, and it means you can document damage the same day.

Repair access is easier too. A damaged panel on a ground mount can often be swapped without moving the rest of the array, and there's no roof to protect or flash. Nobody has to detach the whole system to replace a roof, because there's no roof under it.

## What to check after a hailstorm

Start from a distance and work in. Shut the system off first if you see anything that looks wrong, and read our guide on [how to shut off solar after a storm](/blog/how-to-shut-off-solar-after-storm/) if you've never done it. Don't touch damaged wiring.

**The glass.** Look for cracks, spiderweb patterns, and chips. A panel can look fine and still have problems underneath, since micro-cracks inside the cells don't always show on the surface. Our post on [micro-cracks from hail](/blog/solar-panel-micro-cracks-hail/) explains why a production drop can follow a storm that left no visible mark.

**The frames.** Dents and bent corners can break the seal and let moisture in. Check that panels are still seated properly in their clamps.

**The racking and posts.** Ground mounts rely on steel posts, rails, and braces. Look for bent members, loosened bolts, and posts that have shifted or heaved. Large hail combined with wind can load an array heavily, and the stress goes into the posts and the ground around them.

**The wiring.** Ground arrays usually have conductors running in conduit or a trench to the inverter or the building. Look for exposed or chewed wire, cracked conduit, and any place the ground has washed out or settled.

**The inverter and disconnects.** Check for error codes, dead displays, and anything that tripped. If your monitoring shows the system offline, see our guide to [solar inverter errors after a storm](/blog/solar-inverter-error-after-storm/).

## Hazards that are specific to acreage

Rural properties have a few risks a suburban roof doesn't.

Livestock and equipment. Cattle rub on posts and braces, and horses lean on whatever they can reach. A tractor, a mower, or a baler can clip a corner of the array without anyone noticing. After a storm, check whether damage is from hail or from something with hooves or an engine, because the cause matters for any claim.

Wildlife and rodents. Rodents chew wire insulation, and a ground array near tall grass is an easy target. If you've had a critter problem, our post on a [solar panel critter guard](/blog/solar-panel-critter-guard/) is worth reading.

Wind. Open ground and large tilted surfaces make wind a factor in its own right. Hail and wind often arrive together here, and you may be dealing with both. Our post on [wind damage to solar panels](/blog/wind-damage-solar-panels/) covers what to look for.

Grass and weeds. Overgrowth around a ground array is a fire and shading concern, and it makes damage harder to spot. Keep it trimmed.

## Barn and shop roofs are different

Many acreage owners put the array on an outbuilding, such as a barn, a shop, a machine shed, or a detached garage. That's a roof mount, and it behaves like a house roof.

## Insurance: your policy may file it differently

Here's where ground mounts diverge from house-roof arrays. A roof-mounted array is usually treated as part of the dwelling. A ground mount may instead fall under "other structures" on a homeowners policy, which can carry a separate limit, different terms, and a different deductible setup. An array on a barn or shop might be treated differently again.

We can't tell you how your policy reads, and we're not insurance advisers. Pull your policy, find the other structures section, and ask your carrier directly how a ground array and an outbuilding array are classified. Ask what the limit is and whether it's enough to cover the system. Our overview, [does homeowners insurance cover solar panels](/blog/home-insurance-solar-panels/), goes through the general questions to raise.

When you file, document everything the same day if you can. Photograph each panel, the posts, the wiring, and the inverter, with a wide shot that shows the whole array and the date.

## FAQ

### Do ground mount solar panels get hail damage?

Yes. A ground array takes the same hail as a roof array, and in open country it can take wind-driven hail from the side with nothing to block it. The difference is that ground arrays are easier to walk up to and inspect, so you can document damage quickly after a storm.

### Does a ground mount need a detach and reset?

Not for a roof replacement, because there's no roof under it. A detach and reset applies to roof-mounted arrays, including panels on a barn or shop roof. A ground array only needs to be moved or rebuilt if the racking is damaged or you want it relocated.

### Is a ground mount solar array covered as part of my house?

Not necessarily. A roof-mounted array is usually treated as part of the dwelling, but a ground mount may fall under other structures on your policy, which can carry a different limit and terms. Check your policy and ask your carrier how yours is classified.

### What should I check on my ground array after a hailstorm?

Look at the glass for cracks and chips, the frames for dents, the posts and racking for bent or shifted members, the wiring and conduit for exposed or chewed sections, and the inverter for error codes. Shut the system off if anything looks wrong, and don't touch damaged wiring.

### Can livestock or equipment damage a ground mount array?

Yes. Cattle rubbing on posts, a tractor clipping a corner, or rodents chewing wire can all cause damage that looks like storm damage. It's worth checking the cause before you file anything, since your carrier may ask how the damage happened.
