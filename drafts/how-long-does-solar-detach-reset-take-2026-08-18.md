## The Short Answer: One Day Off, One Day On, Weeks in Between

Let's put real numbers on it before we get into the details.

For a typical Northern Colorado home system, meaning 15 to 30 panels on an asphalt shingle roof, here's what the timeline looks like:

- **Detach day:** 4 to 8 hours. Panels come off, racking comes off, wiring is safed and labeled, everything gets stored.
- **Your reroof:** 1 to 3 days for most homes, though that's your roofer's timeline, not ours.
- **Reset day:** 6 to 10 hours. Racking goes back on with new flashing, panels go back up, wiring is reconnected and tested.
- **Re-energizing:** Same day in most cases. Sometimes a utility or inspection step adds days.

So the hands-on solar work is roughly two working days. The full start-to-finish window is longer because scheduling, roofing, weather, and paperwork all sit between those two days.

The biggest factor in your total timeline isn't the solar crew at all. It's coordination. When the detach, the reroof, and the reset are booked together from the start, the whole job can wrap in under two weeks. Booked separately, six weeks is common and eight isn't rare.

## How Long the Detach Itself Takes

Detach day starts early, usually 7 or 8 a.m., because afternoon wind and summer storms are real constraints on a Northern Colorado roof.

Here's the sequence and roughly how long each step runs on a 20-panel system:

1. **System shutdown and lockout (30 to 45 minutes).** The inverter gets powered down in the right order, the AC disconnect is opened, and the array is verified dead before anyone touches a panel. This step is why detach and reset isn't a DIY job. Panels produce voltage any time light hits them, even disconnected from the grid, and DC arc injuries are no joke.
2. **Panel removal (2 to 4 hours).** Each panel is unclipped, unplugged, carried down, and stacked on edge in a protected spot, usually your garage. Every connector gets capped and every string gets labeled so reset day goes fast.
3. **Racking and rail removal (1 to 2 hours).** Rails, clamps, and mounts come off. We photograph mount locations so the reset lands on the same rafters.
4. **Wire management and documentation (30 to 60 minutes).** Home runs are coiled and protected, penetration points are noted for the roofer, and you get photos of everything as it came apart.

That's one real workday. A bigger system, steep pitch, or tile roof can push it toward the long end or split it across two mornings.

Detach day is also the best chance anyone will ever have to inspect your panels for hail damage, because every panel gets handled up close. If you're reroofing because of hail, ask for that inspection in writing. Our post on [the signs of hail damage on solar panels](/blog/signs-of-hail-damage-solar-panels/) shows what we're looking for, and finding damage while your insurance claim is still open can save you thousands.

## What Happens Between Detach and Reset (and Why It Takes Weeks)

Here's the stretch nobody budgets time for. The panels are in your garage, the roof is getting replaced, and you're not producing power. Every day in this window costs whatever your system would've generated, roughly $2 to $6 a day for most homes here.

Four things fill this gap:

**The reroof itself.** One to three days of actual work for most homes, but roofing crews juggle weather and material deliveries just like we do. After a big hail event, Weld County roofers can be booked out four to six weeks, and your panels sit in the garage that whole time.

**Weather.** We don't set panels in sustained wind above about 20 mph, and nobody roofs in rain. The National Weather Service data for our region shows the Front Range corridor sees some of the most frequent large hail in the country, mostly May through August, which is exactly when most reroofs happen. Check the seasonal outlook at [weather.gov](https://www.weather.gov/) when you're picking dates, because a stormy week can slide everything.

**Roof cure and inspection.** Some roofers want a day or two before anyone walks the new shingles, and some jurisdictions inspect the roof before solar goes back on.

**Scheduling the reset.** If you didn't book the reset when you booked the detach, you're going to the back of the line. This is the most avoidable delay on the whole list. Book both dates at once, every time.

## How Long Does the Reset and Reinstall Take?

Reset day is usually a little longer than detach day, 6 to 10 hours for that same 20-panel system, because it includes everything detach did plus new flashing and full commissioning.

The sequence:

1. **Layout and mounting (2 to 3 hours).** Mounts go back into the rafters, each one flashed and sealed into the new roof. Done right, every penetration gets proper flashing, not just a blob of sealant. Done rushed, this step creates the leaks you find next spring. If you ever see water stains under your array, our post on [roof leaks under solar panels](/blog/roof-leak-under-solar-panels/) covers what went wrong and what to do.
2. **Rails and panels (2 to 4 hours).** Rails get leveled and torqued, then panels go back to their original positions, which is why we labeled everything on detach day.
3. **Wiring and reconnection (1 to 2 hours).** Strings are reconnected per the labels, connectors are inspected, and the home runs are tied back in.
4. **Commissioning (30 to 90 minutes).** The system gets powered up in sequence, string voltages are checked against the original documentation, monitoring is verified, and you get a production reading before the crew leaves.

A reset that skips commissioning is how homeowners end up with one dead string and no idea until the utility bill jumps two months later.

## What Stretches the Timeline (and What You Can Control)

Some delays you can't do anything about. A lot of them you can. Here's the list, sorted honestly.

**Things you can't control:**

- **Weather.** Wind, rain, and hail season all pause roof work. Build a buffer into your expectations from May through August.
- **Utility and inspection steps.** Most straightforward detach and resets in our area don't require utility re-approval because the system isn't changing, just moving off and back on. But if equipment gets replaced during the reset, an upgraded inverter for example, your utility may need to review it. The Department of Energy's homeowner solar guide at [energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/planning-home-solar-electric-system) is a good plain-language reference for how utilities and permitting interact with home solar.
- **Parts availability.** A cracked panel or a cooked connector discovered on detach day can add a week or two if the part has to ship.

**Things you can control:**

- **Book the detach and reset together, with your roofer's dates in hand.** This is the single biggest timeline saver. One phone call, three coordinated dates.
- **Don't let the roofing crew move the panels.** It voids most solar warranties, it's how panels crack, and it's how you end up in a finger-pointing match between two contractors. We wrote up the full picture in [can roofers remove solar panels](/blog/can-roofers-remove-solar-panels/).
- **Get your insurance scope reviewed before work starts.** If detach and reset isn't on the scope, getting it added takes days. Getting it added after the work is done takes months, if it happens at all.
- **Clear garage space before detach day.** A crew that spends 45 minutes rearranging your garage finishes 45 minutes later.

Dave in Johnstown is the cautionary tale here. His roofer's crew pulled his 22 panels themselves to "save him money," stacked them flat on the driveway, and cracked three. Between $1,900 for the replacement panels, a voided workmanship warranty on the rest of the array, and a $2,400 professional reset that his insurer initially balked at because the detach wasn't documented, his shortcut cost him over $4,300 and added a month to his timeline. A coordinated detach and reset would've run him about $2,800, fully on the insurance scope.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can solar panels be detached and reset in the same week as my reroof?

Yes, and that's the ideal setup. A typical sequence is detach Monday, reroof Tuesday through Thursday, reset Friday, which keeps your system down for less than a week. It works when the solar company and roofer coordinate dates before either crew shows up, so share your roofer's schedule when you book.

### How long will my solar system be offline in total?

If the jobs are coordinated, most homeowners are offline four to ten days. If the detach and reset are booked separately or a storm backlog hits, two to six weeks of downtime is common. At typical Northern Colorado production, each offline week costs roughly $15 to $40 in lost generation, so coordination has a real dollar value.

### Does weather delay solar detach and reset work?

It can. Crews won't set panels in sustained winds above roughly 20 mph, and rain or hail stops both roofing and solar work for the day. May through August is hail season on the Front Range, so timelines during those months should include a weather buffer of a few days.

### Do I need a permit or utility approval for a detach and reset?

Usually not, because you're reinstalling the same system in the same location, though some jurisdictions in Weld and Larimer counties want to inspect the roof or the reinstall. If any equipment changes during the reset, like a new inverter or added panels, your utility may need to review the change before you re-energize. A good detach and reset company handles that paperwork and builds it into your timeline up front.
