# How to Shut Off Solar Panels Safely After a Storm

A hailstorm or a windstorm has just gone through, and you can see a cracked panel from the yard. Or a limb came down and something looks off on the roof. Your first thought is probably that you want the system off. That's a reasonable instinct.

This page covers how to shut off solar panels in homeowner terms, what shutting down does and doesn't do, and what to leave alone. System layouts vary a lot, so follow the labels on your equipment and the manual for your system. Treat what's below as the general picture, not a replacement for either.

## What shutting off solar actually does

Here's the part people don't expect. A solar array doesn't have an off switch on the roof. In daylight, panels make voltage whenever light hits them. You can turn off the rest of the system, and the wiring up on the roof can still be live.

Shutting the system down stops power from flowing into your house and out to the grid. It reduces risk, and on newer systems it drops the voltage in the roof wiring to a much lower level. It doesn't make a damaged panel safe to touch.

Keep that in your head the whole way through this page. Shutdown is about calming the system. Staying off the roof and away from broken parts is about you.

## Step one: look, don't touch

Before you flip anything, take a quick look from the ground. You're checking for obvious problems, not doing an inspection.

Stay back if you see any of these:

- A panel that's shattered, hanging, or sliding.
- Wires dangling, cut, or lying on the roof, gutters, or ground.
- Scorch marks, melting, smoke, or a burning smell.
- Water pooled around the inverter or electrical equipment.

If you see smoke or fire, or someone's been shocked, call 911 first. Don't walk across a yard toward downed wire. And never go up on the roof to look closer, especially a wet one with glass on it.

## The shutoff points on a typical system

Most home systems have several places where the system can be turned off. Which ones you have depends on when and how it was installed. Look for labels, because installers are required to mark this equipment.

**The AC disconnect.** This is usually a box with a handle, mounted on an exterior wall near your electric meter or main panel. It's often labeled as a solar or PV disconnect. Switching it to the off position separates the solar system from your house and the grid.

**The solar breaker in the main panel.** Open your electrical panel and look for a breaker marked solar, PV, or inverter. It's typically a two-pole breaker. Switching it off cuts the system's connection from the inside.

**The rapid shutdown switch.** Newer systems have a rapid shutdown device, designed so firefighters can quickly lower the voltage on the roof. It might be a labeled switch near the meter or main panel, and some systems do it automatically when the AC disconnect or main breaker is switched off. On older systems, there may be no rapid shutdown at all.

**The inverter switch.** The inverter, which converts panel power into the power your house uses, often has its own on and off switch. It may be on a wall in the garage or outside. Some systems have a DC disconnect on the inverter too. Follow the instructions for your model, and if it's unclear, don't guess.

If you have a battery, it has its own shutdown steps, and they're different from the panel steps. Check the manual for the right order. Our post on [solar batteries during a roof replacement](/blog/solar-battery-during-roof-replacement/) touches on how batteries fit in.

## A sensible order

If you've decided it's safe to reach the equipment, and it's dry, here's the usual sequence in plain terms. Again, your labels and manual come first.

1. Stand to the side of any box when you operate it, and use dry hands and dry ground.
2. Turn off the inverter, if it has a switch.
3. Switch the AC disconnect to off.
4. Switch off the solar breaker in the main panel.
5. Use the rapid shutdown switch if your system has one.
6. Take a photo of each switch in its new position, so you can tell anyone who comes out later.

If any equipment is wet, scorched, or damaged, or if you aren't sure what a switch does, stop. Leave it, keep everyone away, and make a call.

## What not to do

A few things cause the most trouble after a storm:

- **Don't touch broken panels or loose wiring.** Even with the system shut down, the wires on the roof can carry voltage in daylight.
- **Don't climb on the roof.** Ever, after a storm. Wet surfaces, loose glass, and live wiring are a bad mix.
- **Don't spray water at damaged panels or a fire.** Water and live electrical equipment don't mix.
- **Don't pull or reconnect wires to "fix" something.**
- **Don't restart the system until it's been checked** if there's visible damage.

Shut off doesn't mean dead. If the sun's out, the panels and the DC wiring between them are still energized.

## After the system is off

Once it's shut down and everyone's safe, the next job is documentation. Photograph the damage from the ground, note the time of the storm, and save your last production readings. If you plan to talk to your insurance company, those records help. Don't haul away damaged panels or have anything permanently repaired before they've been photographed.

## FAQ

### Are solar panels still live after I shut the system off?

Yes, they can be. Panels produce voltage whenever light hits them, so wiring on the roof can still carry voltage in daylight after you've switched off the inverter and breakers. Rapid shutdown, where a system has it, lowers that voltage but doesn't make damaged equipment safe to touch.

### Where is the shutoff for my solar panels?

Look for a labeled AC disconnect near your electric meter, a solar or PV breaker in your main panel, a rapid shutdown switch on newer systems, and a switch on the inverter. Layouts vary, so follow the labels on your equipment and the manual for your system. If anything is unclear, leave it alone and call a qualified contractor.

### Should I go on the roof to check the panels after a storm?

No. A wet roof, loose glass, and possible live wiring make it dangerous. Look from the ground, ideally with binoculars, and take photos. A professional can inspect the array safely, and they have the equipment to do it.

### Who do I call if a solar panel is broken after a storm?

Call 911 for fire, smoke, or injury, and call your utility for downed power lines. For cracked panels, damaged racking, or a system that needs to be made safe, call a solar contractor like us. We can come out, secure the array, and give you a written estimate for the repair.

### Can I turn the system back on after shutting it off?

If nothing is visibly damaged and you know why you shut it down, you can usually restore it by reversing the steps in the order your manual describes. If there is any damage, scorching, water, or an error you don't understand, leave it off until a qualified person has checked it.
