# Metal Roof With Solar Panels: Switching From Shingles at Reroof Time

Your shingles are done, and metal is on the table. Maybe a roofer pitched it, maybe a neighbor put one on after a hail year, maybe you've just been thinking about it. Then the question hits: you have a solar array on the roof, so what happens to it?

A metal roof with solar panels is a very workable combination. But switching materials is not a simple swap for the solar side. The panels come off, the metal goes on, and what holds the panels back on has to be matched to the new roof. We'll walk through how that works so you can plan the job and ask the right questions.

A quick note on scope. We don't install roofs and we don't quote roof prices. Your roofer handles the metal. We handle the array: the detach, the storage, and the reset on the new roof.

## Why the metal type decides everything

"Metal roof" covers two quite different systems, and how solar attaches to each is not the same.

**Standing seam metal.** The panels run vertically with raised seams where they join. Because those seams are standing up, special clamps can grip the seam directly and hold the solar racking in place. These clamp-on attachments typically need no holes through the roof at all. That's a real advantage, since fewer penetrations means fewer places for water to find a way in.

**Exposed-fastener metal.** These panels are screwed down through the face of the metal, and the roof has no raised seam to clamp onto. Solar on this type needs a different mount, one that attaches through the metal to the structure below, with proper sealing at each point. It's a different method with different parts.

If you take away one thing, it's this: ask your roofer which type they're proposing, and tell us before the order is placed. The racking that worked on your shingle roof won't automatically work on either one.

## Why a reset on metal usually needs new hardware

On a shingle roof, your array is held up by mounts that were flashed through the shingles into the rafters. When the roof changes to metal, that hardware generally can't just go back where it was.

- **Standing seam** needs seam clamps matched to your specific seam profile. Those clamps are sized to the metal, so the type of standing seam your roofer chooses matters.
- **Exposed-fastener metal** needs mounts designed for that surface, with their own sealing method.
- **The rails themselves** may need to change in length, spacing, or type to fit the new attachment layout.

So the reset on a metal roof isn't a copy of the old one. It's a new attachment design for the same panels. We still do what we do on any reset: inspect the racking, replace corroded hardware, handle the electrical disconnect, recommission the system, and run a production test. But on a material change, expect new hardware to be part of the plan.

## Questions to settle before you order metal

A few conversations now will save you real headaches later.

- **Ask your roofer about the profile.** If it's standing seam, which seam style? Solar clamps are made for specific profiles, and not every one is a good fit.
- **Ask whether they've worked around solar.** Not every metal roofer has, and the coordination matters.
- **Ask about the warranty.** Metal roof warranties can have conditions about what is attached to the roof. Read the document, and see our post on [whether a detach and reset voids a solar warranty](/blog/does-detach-reset-void-solar-warranty/).
- **Ask about timing.** Metal panels are sometimes made to order, so the roof schedule can stretch, and the array sits in storage until the roof is ready.
- **Tell your insurer.** A change in roofing material can matter to your policy. Our post on [home insurance and solar panels](/blog/home-insurance-solar-panels/) lists what to raise.

## Hail and metal roofs

This is a common reason people consider metal in Weld County, so it deserves a straight answer. Metal roofing is often chosen for durability, and many homeowners like that it doesn't shed granules or crack the way shingles can.

But metal can dent. Hail can leave marks on a metal roof, and whether those are a problem or just cosmetic depends on the product and on your insurance policy. Some policies treat cosmetic damage differently, so ask your carrier how yours handles it before you commit.

And the same point applies here as with any roofing upgrade: a tougher roof doesn't make the solar panels tougher. The panels carry their own rating. Panels sold in the US are typically certified to IEC 61215 and UL 61730, and the hail test in those standards uses 25 mm ice balls at about 23 m/s, roughly 51 mph. Bigger hail than that can still crack the glass or the cells. Our [solar hail damage inspection](/solar-hail-damage-greeley/) page explains what we look for after a storm.

## Racking and roof leaks

Any time the attachment changes, the risk that matters is water. On standing seam, clamp-on attachments avoid penetrating the roof, which is the main reason people like them. On exposed-fastener metal, each mount goes through the surface, so the sealing details matter a great deal.

If you've dealt with a leak before, you know why. Our post on [roof leaks under solar panels](/blog/roof-leak-under-solar-panels/) explains where they start. A reset is the cleanest moment to get attachments right, because the roof is fresh and the panels are down.

## FAQ

### Can you put solar panels on a metal roof?

Yes. Standing seam metal usually takes clamp-on attachments that grip the seam without drilling through the roof. Exposed-fastener metal needs different mounts that attach through the surface with proper sealing. Either way, the attachment has to match the specific metal system going on your house.

### Can my existing solar mounts be reused on a new metal roof?

Generally no. The mounts on a shingle roof are flashed through the shingles into the framing, and metal needs a different attachment method. Expect new hardware, and possibly changes to the rails, so the reset is designed around the new roof surface.

### Is standing seam better than exposed fastener for solar?

Standing seam is often easier for solar because clamps grip the seam and typically need no roof penetrations. That's a real advantage for leak risk. Exposed-fastener roofs can still carry solar, but each mount penetrates the metal and needs careful sealing.

### Does metal roofing protect my solar panels from hail?

No. A metal roof may handle hail differently than shingles, but the panels carry their own rating and can still be damaged by large hail. Metal can also dent, which is often cosmetic, so ask your insurer how it treats that kind of damage.

### Do I need a new quote if I switch from shingles to metal?

Yes. A quote written for a shingle roof won't hold once the surface changes, because the attachment type, hardware, and labor can all change. Get a written quote that names the attachment method after your roofer confirms the metal system.
