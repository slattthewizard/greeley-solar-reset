# Signs of Hail Damage on Solar Panels: How to Tell If Your System Took a Hit

Your roof just took a beating, the gutters are dented, and your neighbor already has a roofer's sign in the yard. But your solar panels look fine from the driveway, so they're probably fine, right?

Not necessarily. The signs of hail damage on solar panels are often invisible from the ground, and some of the worst damage never shows up as a crack you can see. Panels can lose 20 percent of their output from internal fractures while the glass looks showroom clean. ## Visible Signs of Hail Damage on Solar Panels

Start from the ground with binoculars or a zoomed-in phone photo. Do not get on the roof after a storm. Wet shingles, loose granules, and damaged decking send homeowners to the ER every hail season, and none of what you're looking for requires being up there.

Here's what visible damage looks like:

**Shattered or spiderwebbed glass.** This is the obvious one. Tempered panel glass doesn't crack in a single clean line. It shatters into a spiderweb pattern radiating out from the impact point, similar to a windshield hit. Even one shattered panel matters, because moisture gets in and corrodes the cells underneath.

**Dents or chips at the panel edges.** The aluminum frame takes hits too. A dented frame can warp the seal around the glass, which lets water migrate into the laminate over the following months.

**Damage to everything around the panels.** This is the tell most people miss. If your metal roof vents are dented, your gutters look like a golf ball driving range, and your AC fins are flattened, your panels took the same hits. Hail doesn't skip the most expensive thing on your roof.

**Cracked or drooping conduit and wiring.** Look at the exposed conduit runs along the roof edge. Cracked conduit means water reaches wiring, and that's a problem that grows quietly.

## Hidden Hail Damage Signs: Micro Cracks and Hot Spots

Now for the damage you can't see, which is honestly the bigger risk in our area.

Solar cells are thin wafers of silicon, thinner than a credit card. When hail hits the glass, the glass can flex without breaking while the cells underneath fracture. These micro cracks are invisible to the naked eye. They don't show on your phone camera. They show up in your production data and, eventually, in physical symptoms.

Watch for these over the weeks after a storm:

**A production drop with no other explanation.** If your system made 38 kWh on sunny days before the storm and now makes 30 on identical weather, something changed. Compare same-month numbers from last year if your monitoring app keeps history. A drop of 10 percent or more after a hail event is the single most reliable indicator of internal cell damage. We break down the mechanics in [solar panel micro cracks from hail](/blog/solar-panel-micro-cracks-hail/).

**Hot spots.** A fractured cell resists current instead of carrying it, and resistance makes heat. Over time this creates a hot spot, a small area of the panel that runs much hotter than the rest. You might see browning or a burn mark on the backsheet, or discoloration under the glass. Hot spots are a fire risk, not just a performance issue.

**Snail trails.** These are thin dark lines that appear on the panel surface months after the damage, caused by moisture reacting with the silver in cracked cells. If you see squiggly discolored lines that weren't there last year, the cracks happened a while ago, often during a storm nobody connected to the panels.

**New inverter errors or ground faults.** Moisture intrusion from a compromised panel or cracked conduit often trips ground fault errors on the inverter. An error code that appears within days of a storm is worth taking seriously.

## Check Your Production Numbers Before You Call Anyone

You have a diagnostic tool most homeowners forget about: your monitoring app. Spend ten minutes with it before you call your insurer or anyone else. Documentation you gather now is the difference between a smooth claim and an argument.

Do this:

1. Screenshot your daily production for the 30 days before the storm.
2. Screenshot the days since the storm.
3. If your app shows per-panel output (Enphase and SolarEdge systems usually do), screenshot the panel-level view. One or two panels reading far below their neighbors is a red flag with a location attached.
4. Photograph your dented gutters, vents, screens, and AC unit from the ground, with a date stamp.
5. Note the storm date and rough hail size. The [NWS hail safety page](https://www.weather.gov/safety/hail) explains how hail size gets reported, and local storm reports back up your date.

Cloudy weeks muddy the comparison, so look at clear-sky days on both sides of the storm date. If the clear-day numbers dropped and stayed dropped, you have evidence, not a hunch.

One thing to be careful about: don't reset your inverter or clear error codes before someone documents them. A cleared error log is evidence you deleted.

## When to Stop DIY and Call a Pro

Everything above the roofline is where your part of this job ends. Here's the honest line between homeowner checks and professional work:

**You can safely do this:** ground photos, binocular checks, monitoring app screenshots, documenting dents on gutters and vents, noting inverter error codes from the display or app.

**Leave this to a licensed pro:** anything requiring roof access, opening the inverter or combiner box, disconnecting or testing panels, thermal imaging, and any repair work. Solar arrays carry live DC voltage whenever there's daylight, even when the system is shut off at the breaker. A damaged array can energize things that shouldn't be energized, which is exactly when an untrained person on a hail-damaged roof gets hurt.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can hail damage solar panels without cracking the glass?

Yes, and it's the most common type of damage we find in Northern Colorado. The tempered glass flexes under impact while the thin silicon cells underneath fracture, creating micro cracks that are invisible from the surface. The reliable symptoms are a sustained production drop, hot spots, or snail trails appearing weeks to months later. A thermal imaging inspection is the way to confirm it.

### How soon after a hail storm should I check my panels?

Do your ground-level photo documentation and monitoring app screenshots within a day or two of the storm. Then watch your production numbers on clear days over the following two weeks. Insurers are far more receptive when damage is documented close to a dated storm event, so early documentation protects your claim even if you file later.

### Will my insurance cover hail-damaged solar panels?

In most cases yes, if the panels are attached to your home and covered under your homeowner policy's dwelling coverage. Hail damage to panels is generally treated like hail damage to the roof, meaning you pay your deductible and the policy covers the rest. Documentation is what makes or breaks these claims, so gather production data and dated photos before your adjuster visit.

### Do my panels need to come off if the roof is being replaced?

Yes, panels have to be fully detached before a reroof and reset afterward, and this labor is commonly included in the roof claim scope. It's also the ideal moment to inspect every panel up close, because each one gets handled individually. If any panels show hail damage, replacing them during the reset avoids paying for a second round of labor later.
