# What Happens to Your Solar Battery During Roof Replacement

You've got a home battery on the garage wall, a roof replacement on the calendar, and a reasonable question: what happens to the battery while the panels are off the roof? Short answer, the battery itself usually stays exactly where it is. The system around it is what changes.

If you've been through the basics already, our explainer on [what a solar detach and reset is](/blog/what-is-solar-detach-and-reset/) covers the roof side. This page is only about the battery, because it's the part of the system homeowners worry about most and ask about least.

## Does the battery come off the wall?

Almost never. Most home batteries, whether it's a Tesla Powerwall, an Enphase battery, or another brand, are wall-mounted in a garage, a utility room, or on an exterior wall. None of that is on your roof, so a roof replacement doesn't require moving it.

What gets disturbed is the connection between the battery and the panels. The array is the source that charges the battery during the day. Take the array down and the battery has nothing to charge from except the grid, and depending on how your system is set up, it may not be allowed to charge from the grid at all.

So plan on this: the battery stays mounted, the roof array comes off, and the whole system gets shut down in a controlled order before any of that happens.

## What the shutdown looks like

A detach starts at the electrical side, not the roof. Before a panel is touched, the system is turned off so the array isn't producing power while wiring is being disconnected. For a home with a battery, that means the battery and its controls are part of the shutdown plan too.

The exact steps depend on your equipment, and manufacturers have their own procedures. We don't improvise those. For anything specific to your battery model, the manufacturer's documentation is the authority, and your battery warranty may have its own say about who is allowed to do what (more on that below).

Once the system is off and the array is disconnected, the panels come down and go into storage. Storage during the reroof is usually included in the detach and reset price. A detach is typically one day.

## Backup power is gone while the panels are off

This is the part to plan around. If your battery is your outage backup, expect that backup to be unavailable, or at least unreliable, while the roof is open.

Think about what the battery was doing for you. During an outage it carries essential loads, and it's refilled by the panels. With the panels off the roof and the system shut down, that cycle is broken. Even if the battery still holds a charge, the system is switched off for safety, and you shouldn't count on it to power anything.

How long does the gap last? It depends on the roofer's schedule more than ours. The roof has to be done, the roofer has to finish, and then the reset happens. We can't promise a date for your roof, but once it's ready, a reset is typically one day and the system is tested and back online the same week as the reset.

A few practical things for the stretch with no backup:

- Check the forecast before the detach is scheduled. Colorado springs and summers can bring storms with little warning, and a house with no backup in a power outage is a different house.
- If anyone at home depends on powered medical equipment, tell your roofer and us early, and make your own plan for that period. A battery is not something to lean on while the system is down.
- Ask your roofer for a realistic start and finish window, then tell us so the reset can be lined up behind it. If it slips, the gap grows.
- Charge phones, flashlights, and anything else you'd normally keep topped up from the battery.

## State of charge and your settings

Batteries have settings: how much charge to keep in reserve for outages, whether they charge from the grid, whether they run on a time-of-use schedule, whether they export to the grid. Those settings live in the battery's app or gateway, and they're worth noting before the shutdown.

Why? Because after a long shutdown the system can come back with defaults, or with a reserve level that isn't what you chose. We'd rather you take a screenshot of your current settings before the detach than rely on memory afterward.

On the state of charge, how a battery behaves when it sits unused for a while is a question for the manufacturer. We aren't going to quote a number or a rule of thumb here, because it varies by product. If you're concerned about it, ask the manufacturer or your battery installer how long the unit can sit off before it needs attention, and whether anything should be done to it beforehand.

## What happens at recommissioning

When the panels go back on the roof, the reset isn't finished at the last bolt. Electrical recommissioning is included in a reset, and for a home with a battery that part carries extra weight.

A system with a battery usually has a gateway or a controller that ties the panels, battery, home loads, and grid together. After the array has been off and on again, that equipment may need to be reconfigured so it recognizes the panels and runs the way it did before. That's where the settings you screenshotted earlier come in.

Our reset covers the following on the solar side:

- New flashed mounts at attachment points, not reused penetrations.
- A look over the racking, with corroded hardware replaced.
- Electrical disconnect and recommissioning.
- A production test to confirm the system is putting out what it should.

If your system has a battery and a gateway that needs manufacturer-level configuration, we'll say so up front rather than after the roof is done. That's a conversation to have before the detach. If your system doesn't come back the way you expect after a reroof, our post on [solar systems that stay offline after roof replacement](/blog/solar-system-offline-after-roof-replacement/) walks through the usual causes.

## Who should touch the battery

Here's the rule we'd give any homeowner: don't let a roofer, a handyman, or a well-meaning relative open the battery or its enclosure, move it, or work on its wiring. Roofers are good at roofs. A battery is high-voltage equipment, and it isn't their trade.

Battery warranties often require that service and certain kinds of work be done by certified installers. We aren't going to tell you what your warranty says, because we haven't read it, and the terms differ by manufacturer and by contract. Pull out the warranty document and look for the sections on service, relocation, and who is authorized to work on the unit. If the language isn't clear, call the company that installed it or the manufacturer and ask directly.

## A simple order of operations

Put together, a reroof with a battery usually goes something like this:

1. Find your battery and gateway documentation and your warranty paperwork.
2. Screenshot or write down the current battery settings.
3. Tell your roofer and your solar contractor that there's a battery, before the date is set.
4. Plan for no backup power from the day of the detach until the system is recommissioned.
5. Shut down the system, detach the array, and replace the roof.
6. Reset the array on new flashed mounts, recommission, and test.
7. Check the battery settings against your notes, and bring in your battery installer if anything's off.

## FAQ

### Does the home battery have to be removed when the roof is replaced?

Usually not. Most home batteries are wall-mounted in a garage or on an exterior wall, so a roof replacement doesn't require moving them. What changes is the system: it gets shut down, the roof array comes off, and everything is brought back online after the reset.

### Will I have backup power during a reroof if I have a battery?

Plan on not having it. With the panels off the roof and the system shut down for safety, the battery can't be counted on to carry your home in an outage. The gap lasts from the detach until the system is recommissioned after the reset, so check the forecast and make your own plan for anything that can't lose power.

### Do I need a certified installer to work on my battery?

Battery warranties often require certified installers for service and certain work, but the terms differ by manufacturer, so check your own warranty document. We handle the panel side of the job and don't claim any manufacturer certification. If your battery or gateway needs manufacturer-level work, that piece should go to a company your warranty allows.

### Will my battery settings reset after the panels go back on?

They can, especially if the system sits shut down for a while or the gateway has to be reconfigured. Take a screenshot of your current settings before the detach, including reserve level and charging options, and compare them after recommissioning. If anything's different, your battery installer or the manufacturer can restore it.

### Does having a battery change the cost of a detach and reset?

The array work is quoted the same way: detach and reset generally runs $200 to $500 per panel, with a typical 20-panel array around $3,500. Anything battery-specific depends on your equipment and on what your manufacturer or installer requires, so it varies and we quote it in writing after seeing your setup.
