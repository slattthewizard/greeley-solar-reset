# Solar System Keeps Going Offline? Check Whether It's the App or the Panels

Your app says the system is offline. Again. If your solar system keeps going offline every few days, the first thing to settle is whether it has stopped producing power or just stopped reporting it. Those are two different problems with two different fixes, and they're easy to confuse.

Most of the time, an offline notice means the monitoring link has dropped, and the panels are quietly working away. This page walks through how to tell, what usually causes repeated drops, and when it's worth a call.

## Offline in the app versus not producing

Your monitoring setup has two jobs. The inverter turns panel power into household power. A separate communication path, usually a gateway or a built-in Wi-Fi radio, sends production numbers up to the cloud so you can see them on your phone.

The first job can be working perfectly while the second one fails. When that happens, the app shows a gap or an offline banner, but your house is still getting solar power. Your utility meter and your bill tell the real story.

Think of it this way. If the app says offline but your inverter is lit and your electric bill looks normal, you have a reporting problem. If the inverter is dark, showing a fault light, or your bill suddenly jumps, you may have a real production problem.

## How to check production without the app

You don't need the app to see whether the system works. Try these in order.

**Look at the inverter.** Most inverters have a status light or small display. A steady healthy light during daylight usually means it's running. A blinking, red, or dark unit deserves a closer look. We won't list light patterns here since they differ by brand, so check your manual or the label on the unit.

**Check the production meter or display.** Some systems have a separate meter or a screen on the inverter showing total energy made. Note the number in the morning and again in the evening. If it climbed, you're producing.

**Compare your utility meter.** On a sunny midday, with few big loads running, a net meter will usually show you sending power to the grid. Your utility's own app or usage history can show whether your daytime draw dropped the way it normally does.

**Look at your bill.** One odd month can be weather. Several months of unexpectedly high bills with no change in how you live is worth chasing. Our post on [solar panels producing less after a reinstall](/blog/solar-panels-producing-less-after-reinstall/) has more on telling a real drop from a reporting gap.

If production is fine and only the app is blank, you have a communication issue.

## Why monitoring keeps dropping

Repeated offline notices usually come from a short list of causes. Most are about the link, not the panels.

**You changed your Wi-Fi router or password.** This is the single most common reason. A new router, a new network name, or a changed password leaves the gateway searching for a network that no longer exists. The system will keep making power. It just can't report it.

**The gateway lost power.** Many gateways plug into an ordinary outlet. A tripped breaker, a power strip someone switched off, a vacuum borrowing the outlet, or a surge can all take it down. If the gateway's lights are off, start there.

**The signal is too weak.** A gateway placed far from the router, on another floor, or behind a garage wall may hold a connection on some days and drop it on others. Anything that changes the signal, like new furniture, a new mesh node, or heavy weather, can tip it over.

**The router is overloaded or restarting.** Some home routers reset on a schedule or get crowded with devices. If the gateway can't reconnect on its own after a reset, it stays offline until someone nudges it.

**Firmware updates.** Gateways and inverters update in the background. During or right after an update, a unit can sit offline for a while or need a reconnect. A single short gap after an update is normal. Repeated gaps are not.

**Cellular or service changes.** Some setups report over a cell connection or through the installer's platform. If the account, the plan, or the platform changed, the data path can break even when nothing on your roof did.

## Simple fixes to try first

Before you call anyone, a few steps cost nothing.

1. **Confirm the gateway has power.** Check the outlet and the breaker. If lights are on, move on.
2. **Restart the router, then the gateway.** Unplug the router for 30 seconds, plug it in, wait a few minutes, then power the gateway off and on.
3. **Reconnect it to Wi-Fi.** If you changed your network name or password, the gateway needs to be pointed to the new one. Your monitoring platform's help pages explain how.
4. **Move it closer.** If you can, try a router or extender nearer the gateway and see if the drops stop.
5. **Wait out an update.** If it dropped once after an update, give it a day before assuming something is broken.

If the app comes back and stays up, that was the whole story.

## When to call

Check production first. Then decide.

Call someone if:

- The inverter is dark, faulted, or clearly not running in daylight.
- Your meter and bill show you're not making power.
- The offline notice began right after roof work or storm damage.
- You've tried the basic fixes and the drops keep coming back.

## Why it drops on some days and not others

An intermittent connection is harder to chase than one that's simply dead. If the gateway holds on for a week and then drops for a day, something at the edge of working is going on. Typical culprits are a signal that's marginal, a router that gets busy in the evening when everyone is streaming, or a gateway outlet that shares a circuit with something that occasionally trips it.

Try noting the time of each drop. Evening drops point toward a crowded network. Drops after storms point toward power flickers. Drops that line up with the router's own restarts point toward the router. You'll often find the pattern in a week.

## What not to do

Don't open the inverter or gateway cover. Don't reset breakers over and over hoping for a different result. Don't assume the worst from one offline day. And don't ignore a long stretch of missing data, since you could be losing production without knowing it.

## FAQ

### Does my solar system stop producing when the app says offline?

Not usually. Offline in the app generally means the reporting link dropped, not that the panels stopped. Check the inverter light, any production display, your utility meter, and your electric bill. If those look normal, the system is making power and only the monitoring is down.

### Why does my solar monitoring go offline after I changed my router?

The gateway is still looking for your old network name or password. It keeps producing power but can't send data until it's pointed at the new Wi-Fi. Reconnecting it through your monitoring platform's setup steps usually fixes it.

### Can a firmware update cause my solar system to go offline?

Yes, briefly. Gateways and inverters update in the background and may sit offline for a while or need to reconnect afterward. One short gap after an update is normal. If it keeps dropping day after day, something else is wrong, such as signal strength or power to the gateway.

### My solar went offline right after my roof was replaced. Is that Wi-Fi?

It might be, but check the inverter and your production first. A detach and reset involves disconnecting and reconnecting the array, and a system that wasn't recommissioned properly can come back dark. If the inverter isn't running in daylight, that's an electrical issue, not a router issue.

### Who should I call when monitoring keeps dropping?

If production looks normal, start with your installer or the monitoring company's support, since it's a communication problem. If the inverter is dark, the drop began after roof work, or you suspect storm damage, call a solar contractor who handles the panels and wiring.
