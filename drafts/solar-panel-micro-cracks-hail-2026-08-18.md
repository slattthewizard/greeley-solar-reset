# Solar Panel Micro Cracks From Hail: The Damage You Can't See From the Ground

That June hailstorm cratered your shingles, dented your gutters, and left your solar panels looking absolutely perfect. Don't trust it.

Solar panel micro cracks from hail are the most common damage we find in Weld and Larimer counties, and they're invisible from your driveway. They're often invisible from three feet away. The glass survives the impact, but the silicon cells underneath fracture like a windshield hit by a pebble, and those fractures quietly eat your production for the next ten years.

If your roof took hail hard enough to need replacement, your panels took the same hits. Here's how micro cracks work, why they matter for your insurance claim, and how to catch them before your adjuster closes the file.

## Why Micro Cracks Are the Sneakiest Hail Damage in Northern Colorado

Hail damage on a roof is obvious. Bruised shingles, exposed mat, granules in the downspouts. Your roofer circles it with chalk and the adjuster approves the scope.

Solar panels play by different rules. The tempered glass on a modern panel is rated to survive 1 inch hail at around 50 mph in lab tests. Northern Colorado routinely throws golf ball and larger hail at us, sometimes wind-driven at highway speeds. The National Weather Service tracks hail up to softball size along the Front Range, and you can read their hail safety basics at [weather.gov](https://www.weather.gov/safety/thunderstorm-hail).

So here's the trap. The glass often holds. Homeowners look up, see no shattered panels, and tell the adjuster the solar is fine. Meanwhile the crystalline silicon cells under that glass, each one thinner than a credit card, have hairline fractures running through them.

Those fractures don't show up in photos from the ground. Most don't show up in photos from the roof either. And once your claim closes without them documented, getting a supplement approved gets a lot harder.

## How Hail Causes Micro Cracks in Solar Panels

A solar panel is a sandwich. Tempered glass on top, a plastic encapsulant layer, then the silicon cells, then a backsheet. The glass is built to flex and spread impact energy. That flex is exactly the problem.

When a hailstone hits, the glass dimples inward for a fraction of a second. The silicon cell underneath doesn't flex. It's brittle, like a ceramic tile. So it cracks in a starburst or branching pattern radiating from the impact point, while the glass springs back to looking factory fresh.

A few things make it worse here in Greeley and the surrounding towns:

- **Repeat storms.** A panel that survived one hailstorm with minor fractures gets hit again the next season. Cracks grow and connect.
- **Wind-driven hail.** Angled impacts concentrate force on panel edges and corners, where cells crack more easily.
- **Temperature swings.** Our 40 degree day-to-night swings expand and contract the cells daily. Existing micro cracks widen a little every cycle.
- **Rough handling during reroofs.** Panels pulled by a roofing crew without solar training pick up new fractures from flexing, stacking, and drops. That's one reason we wrote about whether [roofers should remove solar panels](/blog/can-roofers-remove-solar-panels/) at all.

The result is a panel that passes a glance test and fails a performance test.

## What Micro Cracks Actually Do to Your Production

A micro crack by itself doesn't kill a panel. It isolates slivers of the cell so current can't flow through them. One small crack might cost you a fraction of a percent. The problem is they never stay small, and they never come alone after hail.

Researchers at the National Renewable Energy Laboratory in Golden have studied how cracked cells degrade faster than healthy ones, especially under thermal cycling like ours. You can dig into their photovoltaic reliability work at [nrel.gov](https://www.nrel.gov/pv/reliability.html). The short version: cracked cells lose more power every year than intact ones, and the losses accelerate.

Here's the typical progression we see on Front Range roofs:

1. **Year one:** Production dips 3 to 10 percent. Most homeowners miss it or blame weather.
2. **Years two to three:** Cracks widen. Dead zones grow. Snail trails, those brown squiggly lines, start showing at crack sites as moisture creeps in.
3. **Years three to five:** Isolated cell sections create hot spots, where blocked current turns into heat. Hot spots can brown the backsheet and in rare cases become a fire risk. We covered that in our post on [solar panel hot spots](/blog/solar-panel-hot-spots/).
4. **Beyond:** Bypass diodes fail, whole panel strings underperform, and inverters start throwing errors.

## How to Check for Micro Cracks From Hail Without Guessing

You can't see micro cracks from the ground, and you shouldn't be climbing on a hail-damaged roof to look for something that's invisible anyway. Stay off the roof. Here's what actually finds them, from free to definitive:

**1. Pull your production data.** This is the one thing every homeowner can do safely from the couch. Open your monitoring app and compare the 30 days after the storm to the same window last year, adjusting for obviously cloudy stretches. A drop of 5 percent or more with no other explanation is a red flag. Our guide to the [signs of hail damage on solar panels](/blog/signs-of-hail-damage-solar-panels/) walks through what else to look for.

**2. Check your inverter for new faults.** Ground fault, isolation, or string underperformance errors that started after the storm often trace back to cracked cells or damaged wiring.

**3. Thermal imaging.** An infrared camera, often drone-mounted, spots the hot spots that cracked cells create under load. It's fast and doesn't require touching the array.

**4. Electroluminescence (EL) testing.** This is the definitive test. The panel gets energized in the dark and photographed with a special camera, and cracks show up like bones on an X-ray. It's the evidence adjusters have the hardest time arguing with.

Here's roughly what detection and repair run in our area:

## Micro Cracks and Your Hail Insurance Claim

Colorado insurers see more hail claims than almost anywhere in the country, and adjusters here know roofs cold. Solar is where scopes get thin. A ground-level photo of intact glass often becomes "no solar damage observed" in the report, and the claim closes with your cracked panels still on the roof.

You can push back, but timing matters:

- **Get the panels inspected before the scope is finalized.** Adjusters approve line items far more readily during the original claim than in a reopened one.
- **Insist on evidence, not eyeballs.** Production data, thermal images, or EL photos turn "the homeowner thinks the panels are damaged" into documentation.
- **Use the reroof as your inspection window.** If your roof is being replaced, the panels are coming off anyway. On the ground, in good light, with a trained tech handling them, is the best inspection you'll ever get.
- **Know the deadline.** Most Colorado policies give you 365 days from the date of loss to file or amend hail claims, and some are shorter. Don't sit on a hunch.

## What a Proper Detach and Reset Catches That a Reroof Alone Misses

A [solar detach and reset](/solar-detach-reset-greeley/) is when your panels come off for the roof replacement and go back on after. Done right, it's also a full physical for your solar system, and it's the single best time to find micro cracks from hail.

During a detach and reset, every panel gets handled individually. That means:

- Ground-level inspection of every panel, front and back, in daylight
- Optional EL or thermal testing while panels are off the roof
- Checking every clamp, rail, and flashing point for hail and wind damage
- Inspecting wiring, connectors, and grounding that's been baking under the array
- Documented condition reports with serial numbers, which is exactly what supplements are built on

Compare that to a roofing crew stacking panels in the yard, and the difference isn't subtle. Panels flexed off the rails, leaned against a fence face-down, or restacked without corner protection pick up brand new micro cracks that no storm caused, and nobody's insurance covers careless handling.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can solar panel micro cracks from hail be repaired?

No, not in any practical sense. Once the silicon cell is fractured, there's no way to rejoin it, and the crack will keep growing with every temperature cycle. The fix is replacing the affected panels, which is why documenting the damage for your hail claim matters so much. Panels with minor cracks still produce power, just less of it, so replacement timing is partly an economics question.

### How do I know if my panels have micro cracks if I can't see them?

Start with your monitoring data and compare production before and after the storm, using the same weeks from the prior year. A sustained drop of 5 percent or more is worth investigating. From there, a drone thermal scan or electroluminescence testing gives you a definitive answer, and both are commonly done during a detach and reset when panels are already off the roof.

### Will my insurance cover panels with micro cracks?

Usually yes, if the cracks are documented as hail damage while your claim is open. The challenge is evidence, because adjusters won't pay on a hunch. EL images or thermal scans tied to panel serial numbers, plus production data showing the drop, get supplements approved. Waiting until after the claim closes makes approval much harder, so test before the scope is final.

### Do micro cracks mean my whole system is ruined?

Almost never. Hail damage is usually uneven, so some panels fracture badly while their neighbors test clean. A typical outcome is replacing a third to half the array while the rest goes back up in the reset. That's another reason panel-by-panel testing beats a blanket judgment in either direction, because you shouldn't pay to replace good panels or keep bad ones.
