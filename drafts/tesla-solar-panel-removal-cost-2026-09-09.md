# Tesla Solar Panel Removal Cost: Conventional Arrays and Solar Roof

Tesla solar panel removal cost in Northern Colorado runs about the same as any other array, roughly $200 to $500 per panel, because the badge on the module doesn't change the labor. What moves the number is which Tesla product is actually sitting on your roof.

There are two of them, and they have almost nothing in common. One is a conventional panel array on metal racking, and a lot of those went up under the SolarCity name before that brand was retired. The other is Solar Roof, where the glass tiles are the roof itself.

If you have the first kind, a detach and reset works the way it does on every other system and the pricing below applies to you. If you have the second, you're really having a roofing conversation. ## Two Tesla systems, and only one of them is a panel job

Walk outside and look at the edge of the array from the ground.

A conventional array sits above your shingles. You'll see rectangular modules, usually black or dark blue, floating three to six inches off the roof on aluminum rails, with daylight visible underneath and mounting feet bolted through the shingles at intervals. Plenty of SolarCity-era installs also have a black skirt running along the lower edge to hide that gap. The roof under it is ordinary asphalt shingle.

Solar Roof looks nothing like that. The glass tiles lie flat in courses the same way shingles do, and they are the weather surface. No gap, no rails, no visible mounting feet, and usually no separate roofing material on that plane at all. If you can't slide your hand under the solar, it isn't a panel array.

That one distinction decides who does the work and what it costs. If the term itself is new to you, our plain-language explainer on [what solar detach and reset actually involves](/blog/what-is-solar-detach-and-reset/) covers the mechanics.

## What Tesla solar panel removal cost looks like on a conventional array

Once you've confirmed you have panels on racking, the brand stops mattering much. The sequence is the same [detach and reset we run on any Greeley roof](/solar-detach-reset-greeley/). The crew unbolts the modules from the rails, disconnects and caps the DC wiring, pulls the rails and the mounting feet, and stacks the panels somewhere dry while the roofers work. When the shingles are done, the mounts go back into fresh holes with new flashing, the rails go back up, and the system gets brought online and tested.

Typical Northern Colorado pricing lands like this:

- $200 to $500 per panel for the full round trip
- $1,500 to $2,500 for a small 8 to 12 panel array
- Around $3,500 for a typical 20-panel system
- $6,000 to $8,000 and up for large or complicated arrays

## Ownership comes before price on SolarCity-era systems

A good share of systems from that era are leased or on a power purchase agreement, which means you host the panels but somebody else owns them. ## What's genuinely different about these systems in the field

The panels themselves come off like anyone else's. The friction shows up around them.

Start with the monitoring and gateway hardware. These systems report production through a communications gateway, and when the array is de-energized and reconnected, that link doesn't always come back on its own. A homeowner in Evans can have a perfectly reinstalled array and a blank app for a week because nobody reset the gateway or rejoined it to the home wifi. Restoring monitoring is part of a proper reset, and if yours stays dark, [a system still offline after a roof replacement](/blog/solar-system-offline-after-roof-replacement/) lists the usual causes.

Second, the company that installed your system may not be the company you deal with today. Brands merged, service moved around, and the local crew that set your rails in 2015 may be long gone. Don't plan your reroof on the assumption that the original installer is one phone call away.

Third, and this is the one that actually bites at reset time, the original install paperwork is often hard to lay hands on. A reset crew wants to know the racking manufacturer, the mount type, and the torque spec for the lag bolts. Without documentation, a good crew identifies the hardware on the roof and sources matching parts. A careless one reuses the old feet and the old flashing, and that's how you get a leak six months later.

## What actually drives your number

Panel count is the first lever and the biggest one. After that, three things move the price in a real way.

Access and height matter more than people expect. A single-story ranch in Eaton with a walkable pitch is a straightforward day. A two-story with a steep pitch means fall protection, more staging, and slower panel handling, and all of that lands in the labor line.

Array complexity comes next. Modules split across three roof planes with separate conduit runs take longer than one clean rectangle, and an older string-inverter system has different reconnection steps than a newer setup with optimizers on each panel.

Then there's hardware. A reset done right uses **new flashings and new lag points**, not the old holes. That's a genuine material and labor cost, and it's also the first thing a cheap bid quietly deletes. If one quote comes in well under the others on the same roof, ask in writing what flashing is being installed. We break the whole spread down in [why detach and reset quotes vary so much](/blog/why-solar-detach-reset-quotes-vary/).

One last note on money. On a covered hail claim, the detach and reset is typically paid as a line item in the roof insurance claim rather than out of your pocket. Get it into the scope before the roofer starts, not after.

## FAQ

### Does removing Tesla or SolarCity panels cost more than other brands?

No. Pricing follows panel count, roof pitch, height, and array complexity, not the label on the module. A 20-panel Tesla array on a walkable Greeley roof prices out around the same as a 20-panel array from any other manufacturer, roughly $3,500. What can add cost is hard-to-source mounting hardware on older installs, and that is a parts question rather than a brand premium.

### How do I tell if I have Tesla panels or a Tesla Solar Roof?

Look at the edge of the array from the ground. Panels sit a few inches above your shingles on metal rails, with daylight underneath and mounting feet bolted through the roof. A solar roof has flat glass tiles laid in courses as the actual weather surface, with no gap and no rails. If you cannot slide a hand under the solar, it is not a panel array.

### Can any solar contractor detach and reset a SolarCity system?

Any competent solar crew can handle the physical and electrical work on a conventional array. The real limit is ownership rather than brand. If the system is leased or on a power purchase agreement, whoever owns the equipment decides who is authorized to touch it, so read your agreement and call the provider listed on it before you schedule the roofers.

### Will my monitoring app work again after the reroof?

It should, but it usually needs a deliberate step at the end of the job. The communications gateway loses power during the detach and sometimes does not rejoin the home network by itself. Bringing monitoring back online is part of a complete reset. If your production data is still blank a week after the panels went back up, that is worth a call rather than a wait.

### Is taking the array off permanently cheaper than a detach and reset?

Usually yes, because nobody has to put it back. Permanent removal in Northern Colorado generally runs $1,500 to $3,000 depending on panel count, wiring, and how much patching the roof needs where the mounts came out. That said, an owned array that still produces is worth more on the roof than the removal saves, so run that math first.
