A storm rolls through, and the next morning something doesn't add up. The roof looks fine from the driveway, but a couple of panels are cracked. Or the shingles are bruised and the panels look untouched. Now you're wondering: should I file a hail claim for solar panels when the rest of the house seems okay?

We can't answer that for you, and we're not insurance advisers. What we can do is lay out how the math tends to work, what to gather before you call your carrier, and why getting a written repair quote first usually makes the decision easier. That last part is the piece most people skip.

## Start with your declarations page

Before anything else, find the declarations page of your homeowner's policy. It's usually the first page or two of the policy packet, and it lists your coverage limits and deductibles.

Many Colorado policies carry a separate deductible for wind and hail. Sometimes it's a flat dollar amount. Sometimes it's a percentage of your dwelling coverage, which can be a much bigger number than the deductible you'd pay for a kitchen fire or a burst pipe. Policies differ, and we can't tell you what yours says. The declarations page can.

Two other things to look for while you're there: whether your solar panels are covered as part of the dwelling, or need to be listed separately, and whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. Your agent or carrier can explain how those apply to a rooftop array. Our post on [whether insurance covers solar detach and reset](/blog/does-insurance-cover-solar-detach-reset/) goes through the coverage side in more detail.

## The deductible math in plain words

Here's the basic idea. Your carrier only pays the part of a covered loss that's above your deductible. If the damage comes in under the deductible, a claim pays you nothing, and it may still show up in your claims history.

Let's use made-up round numbers just to show how it works, not to suggest what yours will be. Say your wind and hail deductible were a few thousand dollars, and the panel repair quote came in below that. Filing would likely get you no payment. If the same storm also damaged the roof, the roof and panel losses can combine under that single deductible, and now the total might clear it.

That's the whole reason to think about the roof and the panels together. One storm is usually one claim, with one wind and hail deductible. If the panels are the only damage, the repair has to be bigger than the deductible to be worth filing. If the roof is damaged too, the panel work adds to a claim that's already open.

It also works the other way. If the roof is damaged and the panels are fine, the panels still often have to come off for the roof to be replaced. On a covered hail claim, the detach and reset line is typically paid by the roof insurance claim. We cover the ways that line goes missing in [what to do when the adjuster missed the detach and reset](/blog/insurance-adjuster-missed-detach-reset/).

## Get the repair quote before you decide

This is the step that changes the conversation. Don't try to decide based on a guess. Get a written quote for the panel repair or replacement first, then compare it to your deductible.

A quote gives you a real number to hold against the deductible. It also gives you a document your carrier can read, if you do file. Hail damage to panels varies a lot. A single cracked panel is one conversation. A whole array with hidden damage is another. We don't quote panel replacement prices in a blog post, because it varies with the panel, the count, and the access, and we put it in writing after we've seen the system. Our overview of [hail-damaged solar panel replacement cost](/blog/hail-damaged-solar-panel-replacement-cost/) explains what drives the number.

If a detach is part of the job, those costs are more predictable. Detach and reset generally runs $200 to $500 per panel, a typical 20-panel array comes in around $3,500, and large or complex arrays can reach $6,000 to $8,000 and up. Electrical recommissioning is included in a reset, and storage during the roof work is usually included.

## How to document the damage

Whether you file or not, record what happened while it's fresh. A claim, or even a later conversation with your insurer, goes better with records.

- Take photos of every panel from the ground, plus close-ups of anything cracked, chipped, or bent.
- Photograph hail on the ground, and anything near a coin or ruler that shows size. Dents on gutters, vents, and cars help too.
- Write down the date and time of the storm.
- Screenshot your production in the monitoring app for the weeks before and after the storm. A sudden drop is evidence. Our post on [signs of hail damage to solar panels](/blog/signs-of-hail-damage-solar-panels/) covers what to look for.
- Save any inverter error messages.
- Get a professional inspection. Hidden cell damage doesn't show from the ground.

Don't climb on the roof to take pictures. It isn't worth it, and a wet roof with broken glass on it is a bad place to be.

## When the roof is fine but the panels aren't

This one happens. Shingles and glass don't fail the same way, so a storm can leave the roof in decent shape while the panels above it took the hits, especially on a roof with impact-resistant shingles.

In that case the question is the deductible versus the panel repair quote, and nothing else. If the quote clears the deductible by a comfortable margin, a claim may be worth looking at. If it's close, or lower, filing may leave you with nothing paid and a claim on file. That's your call, and an agent can talk you through what a claim does to your policy. We'd rather you understand the math than have us nudge you either way.

## When the roof is damaged but the panels look fine

Looks can mislead here. Even when the glass is intact, panels can have microcracks under it. If the roof is being replaced, the array has to come off, and that's when it makes sense to have the panels checked. A detach is the chance to see the backs of the panels, the wiring, and the racking.

If you've already got a roof claim open, tell your roofer and carrier that the panels were exposed to the same storm. It's much easier to add panel inspection to an open claim than to go back later. ## Timing and the clock

Policies generally have a deadline for reporting a loss, and Colorado hail season produces plenty of them. We aren't going to quote a number because it varies by carrier. Check the policy, and if you're anywhere near a deadline, ask your carrier directly.

Don't repair anything permanent before you've documented it. Temporary steps to protect the property are fine, but don't have damaged panels hauled away before your carrier has had a chance to look, if you plan to file.

## FAQ

### Is my wind and hail deductible different from my regular deductible?

Often, yes. Many Colorado homeowner policies carry a separate wind and hail deductible, and it's sometimes a percentage of your dwelling coverage rather than a flat amount. Check the declarations page of your policy, or ask your carrier or agent, because the number can be quite a bit larger than your standard one.

### Can I file a claim for just the solar panels?

You can ask your carrier about it, but we aren't insurance advisers and can't say how yours will treat it. Panel-only damage has to be larger than your deductible to produce a payment. A written repair quote tells you whether it is, so get that before you decide.

### What should I document after a hailstorm?

Photograph every panel and any hail on the ground, note the date and time, screenshot your production before and after the storm, and save any inverter error messages. A professional inspection helps because microcracks don't show from the ground. Stay off the roof while you gather this.

### Does it matter if the roof is damaged too?

It can. One storm is often one claim under one wind and hail deductible, so roof and panel damage can add up to clear it where panels alone might not. On a covered hail claim, the detach and reset line is typically paid by the roof insurance claim. Confirm the details with your carrier.

### Should I get a repair quote before calling my insurance company?

For most people, yes. A written quote gives you a real figure to hold against your deductible, and it gives your carrier something concrete if you file. We put our quotes in writing after seeing the system, and we don't guess panel replacement prices without looking.
