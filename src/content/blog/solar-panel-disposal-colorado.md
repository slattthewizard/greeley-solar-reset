You have panels on a driveway, in a garage, or coming off a roof, and you're asking where can I dispose of solar panels without getting it wrong. It's a reasonable thing to worry about. Panels aren't like old shingles or a broken chair, and the right answer depends on whether they still work.

This page covers the realistic options in Colorado, and why the curb or a construction dumpster is usually the wrong call. We're not a recycler, and we don't set the rules, so where rules matter we'll point you to the people who do.

## First question: do they still work?

Everything starts here. A panel that makes power has value. A panel that's cracked, burned, or dead is waste. Sort them before you decide anything.

Panels taken down for a reroof usually work fine. If you're mid-reroof and the plan is to put them back, you don't need disposal at all. That's a detach and reset, not disposal.

Panels that were damaged by hail may or may not be dead. Some are cracked but still produce, which we explain in [does a cracked solar panel still work](/blog/cracked-solar-panel-still-work/). Hot spots, micro-cracks, and glass breaks all affect what you can do with them next.

## Option 1: reuse or resell working panels

If panels still produce, someone may want them. People buy used panels for sheds, cabins, off-grid setups, and small projects. Resale prices are modest and vary a lot, so don't count on this paying for much, but it keeps good equipment out of the waste stream.

A few things to know before you try:

- Panels should be undamaged and clearly labeled with their specs.
- Older or oddball models are harder to place.
- Panels with cracked glass or visible burn marks generally aren't safe to resell.
- If you're selling, be honest about age and condition.

You can list panels locally, ask a solar contractor if they know of buyers, or check with community groups that reuse building materials. A donation or a free pickup is often the easiest outcome for working panels that aren't worth selling.

## Option 2: recycling

Panels that no longer work, or aren't worth reselling, are best sent to a recycler that handles solar. The glass, aluminum frame, and some of the cells' materials can be recovered, though not every facility does all of it.

Recycling for solar isn't as common as recycling for cans or cardboard. Some outfits accept panels, some take them through a mail-in or drop-off program, and some only work with large commercial volumes. We won't name companies or quote fees, because both change and you should get current numbers directly.

Questions worth asking any recycler:

- Do you accept residential panels, and how many?
- Is there a fee, and is that per panel or per pickup?
- Do I need to drop them off, or will you collect?
- Do you take damaged or broken panels? Broken glass can be a handling problem.
- What happens to the panels afterward?

Call a few and compare. Rules and availability shift, and what's true this year may not be true next.

## Option 3: e-waste handling

Some panels, especially older ones or panels with electronics attached, may be routed through e-waste channels. Microinverters, optimizers, and wiring can be handled separately from the glass-and-frame part of the panel.

Local e-waste events and drop-off sites sometimes take solar electronics, but acceptance varies. Don't assume a standard electronics collection will take a full panel. Call first and ask what they'll actually accept.

## Why not the curb or a dumpster?

It's tempting to throw a dead panel in a construction dumpster and be done. Don't do it without checking first.

Some panels contain materials that are regulated when they're thrown away. Lead solder is one example, and certain panel types contain other metals. Depending on testing and state rules, a panel may be handled as hazardous or universal waste instead of ordinary trash. We can't tell you how your specific panels would be classified, because that depends on the panel and on the rules in force.

What we can tell you is that you should check before you toss anything. Contact the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment or your county solid waste office and ask what applies to residential solar panels. They can tell you what's allowed in local landfills and what has to go elsewhere.

There's also a practical side. Waste haulers and landfills may refuse loads with panels in them, and a roofer's dumpster isn't set up for them. Broken glass is sharp, and a panel that's been in the sun is still a live electrical device while light hits it.

## Handling damaged panels safely

A cracked or broken panel can still carry voltage in daylight. Treat it like it's live.

- Don't touch exposed wiring or broken glass with bare hands.
- Stack panels flat, face to face with cardboard between, or lean them securely so they can't fall.
- Keep them dry and out of walkways.
- Wrap or tape visibly broken ones so shards don't spread.

If panels came down after a storm, treat the whole system as live until it has been shut off properly.

## Getting panels ready for pickup or drop-off

Whichever route you pick, a little prep saves trouble. Take photos of each panel and its label before they leave, since the specs on the label are what a buyer or recycler will ask about. Write down how many you have and roughly how old they are.

Keep the panels together and off the ground if you can, on a pallet or on boards, under cover. Wet cardboard and broken glass make a mess, and panels left leaning on a fence can slide. If a recycler wants them wrapped or palletized a certain way, ask before you start so you don't have to redo it.

Don't try to take panels apart yourself. Pulling frames off or scraping glass releases material you don't want to breathe or handle, and it can make a panel harder for a recycler to accept.

## FAQ

### Can I put old solar panels in the trash in Colorado?

Don't until you've checked. Some panels contain materials like lead solder and may be treated as hazardous or universal waste depending on testing and state rules. Contact the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment or your county solid waste office to find out what applies to your panels.

### Can I sell or give away panels that still work?

Often, yes. Working, undamaged panels have value for sheds, cabins, and small projects. Prices are modest and vary by age and model. Panels with cracked glass or burn marks shouldn't be resold. If a sale isn't worth the trouble, a free pickup or donation is a common outcome.

### Do recyclers accept residential solar panels?

Some do, and some only take large commercial loads. Availability, drop-off rules, and fees change, so call a few and ask whether they accept residential panels, how many, whether damaged panels are okay, and what they charge. Get current numbers directly instead of relying on old information.

### How should I store damaged panels until disposal?

Treat them as live in daylight. Stack them flat with cardboard between, keep them dry and out of walkways, and wrap visibly broken panels so glass doesn't spread. Don't touch exposed wiring or broken glass with bare hands.
